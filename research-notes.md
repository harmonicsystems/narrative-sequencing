# Research notes: narrative intervention and this tool

What the narrative intervention research suggests, how this tool supports it,
and what the tool deliberately leaves to the clinician. Written November 2026
from Language Dynamics Group's public pages
(https://languagedynamicsgroup.com/research/) and their 2025 *Story Champs
Research Synthesis*.

Story Champs® is a commercial program from Language Dynamics Group. This tool
is not affiliated with it. We borrow general principles from the published
research, not its stories, icons or materials.

## What the research names as effective

The synthesis lists these practices:

- **Story grammar.** Stories built from character, setting, problem, feeling,
  action, ending and end feeling. Later levels add a plan, a consequence and a
  second episode.
- **Multiple exemplar training.** Many short stories with the same parts, so
  the structure, not one story, is what is learned.
- **Visual materials with systematic fading.** Pictures and story-part icons
  at first, then less, then none.
- **Retrieval through retelling,** followed by **personal story generation**
  (the student's own story with the same parts).
- **Complex academic language.** Temporal connectives (*when*, *after*) and
  causal connectives (*because*, *so that*), plus vocabulary in context.
- **Frequent chances to respond,** modelling and recasting, and immediate
  corrective feedback.
- **Low dose.** Sessions of 10–45 minutes, 1–5 times a week, over 2–72 weeks.
  Many studies used twice a week for ten weeks.

## How this tool maps to it

| Practice | In this tool |
|---|---|
| Story grammar | `card.part` names each card's part. Four stories are built this way: `kite-in-the-tree`, `spilled-paint`, `lost-library-book`, `block-tower`. |
| Multiple exemplars | Those four share the same shape: character → problem → feeling → action → ending. |
| Fading visual support | Retell support: **Pictures → Words only → No cards**, chosen by the clinician and logged in order. |
| Retell | The retell step marks parts told and prompted, and the order told. |
| Connectives | Separate counts for time words and cause words. |
| Corrective feedback | Kept deliberately light: "Try again", then the order is shown. Cues come from the clinician, never the app. |
| Personal story generation | Not in the app. The clinician can follow a retell with "Tell me about a time…" using the same parts. |

## What the tool does not do

- It does not score story grammar the way a standardized measure does (for
  example the 0/1/2 element scoring of narrative retell measures). It records
  which parts the clinician marked as told, and nothing more.
- It does not decide whether a part was told completely or clearly. That is
  the clinician's judgment.
- It is not an assessment and gives no norms, benchmarks or cut scores.

## Caveats

- Most published Story Champs studies are by the program's developers
  (Spencer, Petersen and colleagues). The synthesis lists a smaller set of
  independent studies and reviews.
- The evidence is concentrated in preschool to grade 3 and is thinner for
  grades 4–8, which this tool also serves.
- The synthesis shows effect sizes as bars without numbers. Look up the
  individual papers before quoting a size.
